## Position

- Postdoctoral Researcher at the **[Oxford Centre for Human Brain Activity (OHBA), Department of Psychiatry, University of Oxford](https://www.win.ox.ac.uk/about/our-locations/OHBA)**

## Research
My research uses wearable OPM-MEG to investigate brain activity in real-world, naturalistic settings. I develop novel scanning setups and analysis pipelines to extract high-level insights from complex neural datasets. At Oxford I led the installation and testing of a new Neuro-1 OPM-MEG system and shielded room, in collaboration with Cerca Magnetics Ltd.

I am particularly interested in the neuroscience of social interaction and communication, and the role of synchronised neural oscillations during two-way exchanges. This involves scanning developing neurotypical participants as well as autistic participants, with the aim of understanding more about social interaction in neurodivergent populations. I am currently using a movie-watching paradigm with OPM-MEG to study the oscillatory power and dynamic network changes underlying social brain activity.

To capture how people move during naturalistic experiments, I developed **[CamKit3D](https://github.com/neurofractal/camkit3D)**, an open-source markerless motion-capture system that turns a handful of ordinary USB webcams into a 3D motion-capture setup, and which can be synchronised with wearable OPM-MEG.

Additionally, I am interested in the use of virtual and augmented reality in neuroscience research. During my previous postdoc at UCL (2020-2024) we pioneered the use of OPM-MEG in combination with virtual reality to study the neural basis of autobiographical memory encoding.
